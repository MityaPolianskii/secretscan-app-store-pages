An identity-document photo audit

That ID photo may have outlived its purpose.

Passport, driver's-license, national-ID, and insurance images often begin as temporary application or travel copies. This guide helps you find the copies, decide what is still needed, and reduce unnecessary exposure.

Start with what iPhone already provides

Look in Photos Utilities and Search

  1. Open Photos, tap Collections, then scroll to Utilities.
  2. Open Documents to review images Photos has grouped as documents. This is useful, but it is not guaranteed to contain every ID copy.
  3. Tap Search and try context visible in the image: passport, driver's license, national ID, identity card, the issuing country or state, your surname, or a recognizable heading.
  4. Review Screenshots, Recently Saved, and Recently Shared when those collections match how the copy was created or used.

Apple says Photos can group document images in Utilities and search text visible inside photos. Results still depend on recognition quality and what you remember. A document may have no searchable English label, and a generic application screen may match without containing an actual ID.

Use a private pattern scan for a second pass

SecretScan uses Apple's on-device OCR and deterministic checks for likely labeled passport, driver's-license, national-ID, tax-ID, and related document numbers. It ranks candidates for human review; it does not upload Photos or extracted text to SecretScan servers.

The scan is intentionally not a promise that every ID image will be found. OCR can miss glare, blur, small print, unfamiliar layouts, or unsupported labels. Pattern checks can also flag harmless examples. Confirm the entire image yourself before taking action.

Decide whether the copy is still necessary

  • Temporary upload completed: verify the application, rental, travel, or verification process no longer needs the local copy.
  • Copy still required: keep only what is necessary in storage you deliberately chose and protect. Do not delete the only required travel or recovery document without a verified alternative.
  • Supported digital ID: Apple Wallet can securely hold eligible IDs in participating regions, but availability is limited and it may not replace the physical document. Adding an ID to Wallet does not remove an old image from Photos.
  • Sharing copy: disclose only the fields the recipient actually requires. A cropped or redacted copy is separate from the original, which may still remain in Photos.

Remove copies deliberately

  1. Delete obsolete originals only after confirming that any necessary replacement is usable.
  2. Review Recently Deleted. Apple retains deleted photos there for 30 days unless you permanently remove them sooner.
  3. Review which apps can access Photos under Settings → Privacy & Security → Photos, and remove access an app no longer needs.
  4. Check the message, shared album, support ticket, cloud drive, or other destination where the image was sent. Deleting your local copy does not retract another copy.

Hiding an image moves it to the locked Hidden collection. That may reduce casual visibility, but it does not remove the document copy from Photos.

If the information may be misused

A photo copy existing on your phone is not the same as a lost or stolen physical document. If the original document is actually lost, stolen, or used without permission, contact the issuing authority and follow its instructions. In the United States, IdentityTheft.gov provides situation-specific steps for exposed driver's-license and passport information and a recovery plan when identity theft occurs.

If you merely sent an ID image to an expected recipient, first confirm that recipient's retention and deletion process. SecretScan cannot revoke an identity document, contact a recipient, or determine whether identity theft occurred.
